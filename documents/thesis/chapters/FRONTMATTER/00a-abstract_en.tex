Intrinsic image decomposition (IID) tries to separate a photograph into two layers:
the \emph{albedo}, which describes the reflectance of each surface, and the \emph{shading},
which describes how light falls on those surfaces.
A single photograph cannot tell a dark surface from a dimly lit one, or a warm surface
from a white surface under warm light, so part of the lighting leaks into the recovered
albedo. The same wall then comes out lighter or darker, and sometimes tinted, depending on
how the room was lit, although the paint has not changed.

We train against this leakage with \emph{Cross-Illumination Albedo Invariance} (CIAI).
Two photographs of the same scene under different illumination pass through the same
network; an invariance loss requires their predicted albedos to agree, and an explanation
loss requires the ratio of the two predicted shadings to match the luminance ratio of the
two images. The idea follows Siamese representation learning and multi-view consistency in
3D reconstruction, with positive pairs only. No dataset offers real photographs under
several illuminations together with dense measured albedo, so we use the Multi-Illumination
Dataset (MID), in which every scene is photographed 25 times with a flash bounced in a
different direction, and the pseudo-ground-truth albedo that MIDIntrinsics derives for all
25 frames. CIAI is added as a second training stage to a model with a frozen DINOv2-Large
encoder, a DPT decoder and three-channel shading; inference remains a single image and a
single forward pass.

In matched ablations CIAI reduces the variation of a material's recovered lightness across
illuminations ($C_{\text{mat}}$) by $28\%$ and $39\%$, and on synthetic ARAP renderings with
their original coloured lighting it also lowers the dense albedo error by $7$--$14\%$. Our
model is more lightness-stable than CD-IID, both Marigold variants and Ordinal Shading, level
with CRefNet and behind RGB$\rightarrow$X, while training $18.5$M parameters.
CIAI does not make the albedo stable in \emph{colour}. MID's flash is white: between two
frames the light changes mainly in direction and intensity, and its chromaticity moves by a
median of only $0.030$. The explanation loss, moreover, is defined on luminance. Under CIAI
the scale-normalised hue drift improves by only $4$--$6\%$, and our model's hue drift still
grows with the illuminant colour as much as that of a grey-shading method. CD-IID, which
predicts the shading colour in a dedicated stage, leads both hue drift and chroma accuracy.
Two follow-up studies (a chroma explanation loss on the existing shading, and rendered
coloured-light pairs) do not change this, and we use them to argue that colour invariance needs a structural
change: shading split into a luminance and a chroma factor, a chroma explanation loss on
the chroma factor, and training pairs whose illuminant colour genuinely varies.

Two further findings shape the evaluation. The desaturated albedo of the base model is
caused by the frozen-encoder architecture, not by CIAI: a colour path restores the chroma
spread with or without CIAI. And a chroma variance pooled over all materials, which we used
in an early draft, rewards desaturated predictions because it contains the scene's own
colour diversity; we therefore report stability only next to accuracy against measured
albedo (MAW) and against the MID pseudo-ground truth.
